# Axis: literature

Configs tested: 87 (VAL checked: 4). Holdout (2024+) not used.

## Recommendation

No robust edge was found on the literature axis. Treat none of the three leads as a finding. Design implications for the gold session-breakout EA, ranked:
(1) Cost per R decides everything. The Asian breakout's gross follow-through (+0.096W, race P1W 0.535) about equals the round-trip cost (0.092W). Every filter should be judged net of costs and under cost stress. Prefer wider ranges or stops (min_w_atr around 0.32-0.39 is the only lead that held on VAL: IS +0.069R t1.57, VAL +0.123R t1.29). Never use tight ATR-fraction stops of the Zarattini 5%-ATR kind: with a 0.47 USD round trip they cost more than 0.5R.
(2) Do not add Crabel NR4/NR7 filters on the Asian range; narrow Asian ranges are the worst days (net -0.14W).
(3) Keep long and short rules symmetric. Gold's 2014-2021 rise happened in Asian hours (00-07 London +2.26 bps/day, t 2.8); the 07-20 trading window has a slight negative drift. Any long-only or trend-long bias in London/NY is not supported by drift.
(4) Relative-activity or relative-volume filters, the heart of the published ORB successes, are not robust on gold with stops and costs. Any agent testing them must measure activity strictly before the entry; overlapping windows produce spectacular look-ahead results.
(5) Intraday momentum is real but about 0.7 bps/day, so it only justifies holding winners toward the session end (no tight TP). It does not justify a separate last-half-hour strategy.
(6) Ignore London-fix direction; the bias ended with the March 2015 IBA auction. Expect volatility spikes at 13:20/13:30 and 15:00 London, so avoid stop placement or entries that straddle them with tight stops.
(7) The simple 'trade the NY open instead of London' version (London-morning range broken 13-17 London) also has gross follow-through below cost (0.040W vs 0.076W).
(8) Expect decay. The follow-through probability fell from 0.615 (2015) to 0.458 (2021) within IS, consistent with the documented decay of ORB and technical-rule profits. A holdout pass on 2024+ should not be expected to beat IS.

- VERDICT: no robust edge was found on this axis. The literature explains why the naive gold Asian breakout is flat, but no published effect converts into a robust net edge after costs on 2014-2021 IS data.
- CORE MECHANISM, costs versus follow-through. Published ORB edges assume costs that are tiny relative to R: Zarattini & Aziz 2023 get +0.13R per trade on QQQ with no slippage and no spread. On gold the default Asian breakout (range 00-07 London, breaks 07-12) has a real but small gross follow-through. The race P(+1W before -1W) from the broken level is 0.535 (z 2.71, n 1470 resolved; a random walk gives 0.5). The gross move from the level to the 20:00 close is +0.096W (t 2.23). The round-trip cost is 0.092W (0.47 USD / W), so the net is about 0; engine default IS +0.0078R. Costs per R are the binding constraint.
- INTRADAY MOMENTUM (Gao et al 2018 JFE; Baltussen et al 2021 JFE) is present in spot gold but too small to trade. The Gao test (first half-hour of the COMEX pit, measured from the prior 13:30 ET close, predicting 13:00-13:30 ET) gives slope 0.017, t 2.17, R2 0.84%, and a timing return of +0.68 bps/day (t 2.35), positive in 8/8 years. That is about 0.09 USD/oz against a 0.47 USD round trip. The Baltussen rest-of-day test gives t 3.13, R2 1.2%, and R2 4.1% in the tercile with the most active first 30 minutes of NY. London first 30 min predicting the rest of the day: t 1.68, R2 0.23%. The literature agrees commodity intraday momentum is tiny (Baltussen: pooled commodity R2 0.09-0.13%). For GLD, the first half-hour does not predict at all (Xu et al 2020).
- RELATIVE VOLUME / 'STOCKS IN PLAY' (Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz 2024: below 1x relative volume gives -0.02R, above 30x gives +0.38R; all-stock ORB Sharpe only 0.48) does not transfer robustly. HistData volume is all zeros, so the proxy was relact_lon = range(07:00-07:30)/14-day mean. Gross results look strong for Asian breaks at or after 07:30 in the top tercile: P1W 0.58, follow-through 0.31W (t 2.78), n 231. With the engine's 1W stop and costs it is not robust: thresholds 1.0/1.25/1.5 give +0.065/-0.069/+0.087R (t 0.69/-0.62/0.49). A striking NY version (P1W 0.586, 8/8 years) was LOOK-AHEAD, because the activity window overlapped the breakout minute. For breaks after the window, n 76 and the follow-through is -0.02W.
- COMPRESSION -> EXPANSION (Crabel 1990 NR4/NR7) is wrong-signed for the gold Asian range. Narrow ranges expand more only in their own units: post-range/W is 3.5 in W/ATR quintile 1 vs 1.3 in quintile 5, Spearman -0.61. In ATR units they do not expand (0.76 vs 0.89). Their directional follow-through is not better: Asian NR7 P1W 0.493, Q1 0.543. Their cost/W is highest (0.13-0.14), so net is worst: Q1 -0.136W, NR7 -0.147W. W/ATR Q4 has P1W 0.584 and net +0.159W (t 2.11). This favours a MIN width filter, i.e. the opposite of Crabel.
- VOLATILITY STATE (Lundstrom 2014 says ORB returns rise in high-volatility states, measured in bps). In R terms gold shows the reverse. The calm regime (ATR% <= 0.85 x its 250-day median) gives P1W 0.604 (z 3.44) and engine IS +0.164R (t 1.92, 6/8 years, symmetric long/short), but VAL -0.032R, so it FAILED. The turbulent quintile has net -0.196W.
- TIME-OF-DAY DRIFT (Blose & Gondhalekar 2014/2018: gold overnight > 0, COMEX day < 0). Gold's 2014-2021 rise happened in Asian hours: 00-07 London +2.26 bps/day (t 2.83, 6/8 years); 23-07 +3.58 (t 4.93, 8/8, but this includes a rollover artifact). The 07-20 London engine window is -1.56 bps/day (t -0.93, 3/8 years positive). COMEX pit day is -1.29 (t -0.97) and night +2.12 (t 1.86): same signs as the literature but not significant. 07:00-08:00 London is -1.05 bps (t -2.78, 1/8 years positive). Implication: a London/NY breakout gets no bull-market tailwind for longs. Engine default longs -0.008R vs shorts +0.026R; race P1W long 0.522 vs short 0.548. Keep the rules symmetric.
- LONDON FIX. Abrantes-Metz & Metz (downward PM-fix moves in 2004-2013) and Caminschi & Heaney 2014 JFM (information leakage in the first minutes of the fix) are visible only in the old fixing regime. Before 2015-03-20 (n 313): 14:55-15:15 -2.51 bps (t -2.44), 69% of top-decile moves down, and 15:00-15:05 -1.67 (t -2.45). After the IBA auction (n 1752): -0.54 (t -1.20) with 57% down vs a 56% control, and 15:00-15:05 -0.03. The effect is gone, so do not use fix direction. The volatility spikes remain: mean abs 1-min return 6.6 bps at 13:30 London (8:30 ET data), 5.2 at 15:00, 4.35 at 13:20 (COMEX open), against a median minute of 1.37.
- SESSION CHOICE ('trade the NY open, not London'). The literature supports NY as the locus of price discovery and volatility (Hauptfleisch, Putnins & Lucey 2016; Sobti et al 2021: NY about 56% information share; Elder et al 2012 and Cai et al 2001: 8:30 ET releases dominate). Gold volatility is 1.7x average in 13-16 London and about 1.0x at the London open. But a simple London-morning range (07-13) broken 13-17 gives P1W 0.541 (z 2.67) and gross follow-through 0.040W (t 1.34), below its cost of 0.076W. Asian-range breaks at 08:00-11:59 follow through more than breaks at 07:00-07:59 (0.165W vs 0.052W, P2W 0.579 vs 0.520), but restricting engine entries to >= 07:30 gives -0.008R.
- STOP-HUNT / LIQUIDITY-SWEEP claims have no peer-reviewed support. The credible evidence (Osler 2003 JF and 2005 JIMF; Kavajecz & Odders-White 2004 RFS) says stop-loss clusters beyond levels cause continuation cascades and take-profit clusters cause reversals. On gold, after an Asian-range break, P(price returns to the range mid before reaching +0.5W) is 0.469 against a 0.5 null (z 2.6 toward continuation). Breaks slightly continue on average; systematic sweeps are not supported.
- DECAY. The literature documents ORB and technical-rule decay: the 'Crabel' century draft (gross, author unverified) reports Sharpe above 6 in the 1970s-90s, 2.92 in the 2000s and 0.91 in the 2010s; Neely, Weller & Ulrich 2009 find FX rule profits vanished; Holmberg et al 2013 find crude-oil ORB profits concentrated in 2000-2011. Gold shows the same within IS. P1W by year: 2014 0.528, 2015 0.615, 2016 0.567, 2017 0.554, 2018 0.543, 2019 0.503, 2020 0.520, 2021 0.458.

## Engine / data notes

- REPORT.md not written: the Write tool refused explore/literature/REPORT.md ('Subagents should return findings as text'). The full content is in key_findings and candidates. The scripts and out_s0*.txt result files are on disk under explore/literature/.
- Data: the volume column in XAUUSD_M1_BID.parquet is all zero for 2014-2021 (HistData has no volume). Relative-volume filters from the ORB literature (Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz 2024) cannot be replicated; only range-based proxies can be used. Reproduce: pd.read_parquet('data_cache/XAUUSD_M1_BID.parquet', columns=['volume']).query('index < "2022-01-01"').volume.abs().max() == 0.
- Data caveat, probable BID-quote artifact at the daily rollover. IS mean BID log return by London hour: 21:00-22:00 is -1.21 bps/day (t -5.36, positive in 1/8 years) and 23:00-24:00 is +2.06 bps/day (t +5.62). This is consistent with BID quotes falling as spreads widen into the 22:00 halt and recovering after the reopen. The hourly-median spread model (constant within an hour, ASK = BID + spread) cannot represent this widening, so strategies that enter or exit between 21:00 and 23:00 London are mismeasured. Minute-level volatility also spikes at 17:15 and 18:00 ET (22:15/23:00 London) on days with bars inside or at the end of the halt. Reproduce with explore/literature/s01_timeofday.py (Check A1).
- Methodology trap, not an engine bug: relative-activity (or relative-volume) conditioning whose window overlaps the breakout minute creates strong spurious results. In IS, a London-morning range (07-13) broken 13-17 with the COMEX first-30-min (08:20-08:50 ET) activity in the top tercile gives race P1W 0.586 and 8/8 years positive. Restricting to breaks after the window gives n 76 and gross follow-through -0.02W. Other agents testing activity or volume filters should check this.
- Minor guard suggestion: engine.load() returns arrays through the end of the data, including the sealed holdout. Descriptive work on D arrays must slice manually; this axis cut at 2022-01-01 via searchsorted. An engine.load(until=...) or an assertion helper would reduce the risk of accidental holdout reads. No engine logic bug was found in the paths used (default Params and min_w_atr).
